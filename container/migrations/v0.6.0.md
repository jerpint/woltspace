# v0.6.0 — apps and sites get their own door, the lodge checks its own login

**Required?** Partly. Nothing breaks on this machine, and nothing breaks for
most lodges. But some steps change what people outside this machine can reach,
and the human has to decide those. Walk through them in order; each one says
whether it applies.

**Coming from:** any 0.5.x lodge (native or container).

**Who acts:** you (the wolt) read each step aloud, check whether it applies, and
do only what the human agrees to. Values like an email address, an Access
audience or a domain come from the human; never guess them. None of them is a
secret, so they may be said in chat.

## What changed

- **Apps have their own gateway.** Apps are served by a separate small server
  on the lodge port minus 660 (7117 for a lodge on 7777), with none of the
  lodge's routes. Remotely, apps live on their own domain behind their own
  Cloudflare Access application. On this machine nothing changes:
  `http://<app>.localhost:<lodge port>` still works.
- **Apps start on loopback.** Apps are started with `HOST=127.0.0.1` and
  `HOSTNAME=127.0.0.1`, and an app with no `start` command (a static folder)
  is no longer served.
- **Nothing is public by default.** `"public": true` in an app's
  `woltspace.json` is ignored. An app is shared on purpose: by its share
  list, or by an opt-in quick tunnel (`WOLTSPACE_APP_QUICK_TUNNELS=1`, which
  replaces `WOLTSPACE_SHARING_ENABLED`).
- **The lodge is never quick-tunnelled.** A container lodge without a named
  tunnel used to get a random public address. It now stays on this machine.
- **The lodge can verify Access itself.** With Access settings saved
  (`woltspace access set`), the lodge checks every remote request's token for
  the owner's email, instead of only trusting the edge. Without them it keeps
  trusting its way in, and `woltspace doctor` says so.
- **Wolt sites have their own address.** Each wolt's site is served by the
  app gateway on its own origin, like an app: `<wolt>.sites.localhost:<gateway
  port>` on this machine. Old `/wolt/<name>/site` links redirect there, and the
  wolt page and split view open it directly. Only the owner sees a site unless
  it's shared (`woltspace site share <wolt> <email>`). Livereload works there.
  Remote site addresses (`site-<wolt>.<app domain>`) are not served yet and
  answer "pending setup".
- **Removed:** the desktop app, the first Telegram adapter, a one-off session
  migration script, and `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Steps

### 1. Take stock

Run `woltspace paths` and `woltspace doctor`, and read the data root's `.env`
keys (names only, never print values) and `woltspace.json`. Note:

- Is the lodge published? (`CLOUDFLARE_TUNNEL_TOKEN` and
  `CLOUDFLARE_TUNNEL_URL` set, and `WOLTSPACE_PUBLIC_TUNNEL` not `false`.)
  If doctor says "CLOUDFLARE_TUNNEL_TOKEN is configured but the tunnel is
  disabled", the human turned publishing off on purpose: the lodge is **not
  published**. Note it and move on; don't follow that line's remedy unless the
  human asks to publish.
- Does `woltspace.json` have an `access` block?
- Which apps exist under `wolts/apps/`, and which have `"public": true` or no
  `start` command?
- Is `WOLTSPACE_SHARING_ENABLED` set?

Tell the human what you found in a few lines before changing anything.

### 2. Let the lodge verify its own login (published lodges)

Applies if the lodge is published and has no `access` block. Today the lodge
trusts whoever its tunnel lets through. If Cloudflare Access protects the
tunnel's hostname that is the owner, but nothing in the lodge checks it.

Recommend turning the check on. It needs three values, none of them secret:

- the **team domain**, ending in `.cloudflareaccess.com`
  (Zero Trust → Settings → Custom Pages, or the API's
  `accounts/<id>/access/organizations` → `auth_domain`);
- the lodge application's **Application Audience (AUD) tag**
  (Zero Trust → Access → Applications → the lodge app → Overview, or the
  API's `access/apps` entry whose `domain` is the lodge's hostname → `aud`);
- the **owner email** the Access policy allows.

The cloudflare skill's `setup.md` (Step 5) has the exact API calls if
`CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` is in `.env`. Then:

```bash
woltspace access set --team-domain <team>.cloudflareaccess.com \
  --lodge-aud <AUD tag> --owner-email <owner email>
```

Leave `--apps-aud` empty unless step 4 sets up an app domain. Check the result
from outside: the human opens the lodge on their phone and signs in as usual.

**If it locks the human out** (wrong audience or email): on this machine,
`woltspace access set` again with the right values, or `woltspace access clear`
to go back to trusting the tunnel. On a headless host, do it over SSH. This
machine always reaches the lodge, whatever the settings say.

If the human prefers to keep trusting the tunnel, that is their call. Say once
that anyone the tunnel lets through is treated as them, and move on.

### 3. Apps that were public

Applies to apps with `"public": true`. They are no longer shared when they
start. For each one, ask the human who should reach it:

- **Specific people:** add their emails to the app's share list
  (`woltspace app share <app> <email>`); they reach it through the app domain
  (step 4).
- **Anyone with a link, no login:** set `WOLTSPACE_APP_QUICK_TUNNELS=1` in
  `.env`, restart the lodge, and open a quick tunnel for that app through the
  share API. Say plainly that a quick tunnel has no login.
- **Nobody else:** nothing to do.

You may remove the `"public"` key from the manifest. It is ignored either way.
(The Apps page badge reflects quick tunnels, not the share list, so it still
says "Just me" after sharing by email. That's expected.)

If `WOLTSPACE_SHARING_ENABLED` is set, it no longer does anything: replace it
with `WOLTSPACE_APP_QUICK_TUNNELS` only if the human wants quick tunnels.

### 4. An app domain (only if apps are opened from outside this machine)

Applies if the human opens apps from a phone, or shares them. Remote app links
show "Apps are served on the app domain" until it exists. It is a one-time
setup with the cloudflare skill's `app-domain.md`: a separate domain, a
wildcard DNS record, one tunnel ingress rule for the gateway port, and a
Cloudflare Access application for apps. That application's AUD tag goes in
`woltspace access set --apps-aud …` (repeat the other values from step 2).

### 5. App start commands bind to loopback

Applies to **every** app. 0.6 starts apps with `HOST=127.0.0.1`, but that is
only a hint: a command that ignores it (e.g. `python3 -m http.server $PORT`,
the usual 0.5 start command) still listens on all interfaces. Read each app's
`start` command and add an explicit loopback bind where the tool supports one
(`--bind 127.0.0.1`, `--host 127.0.0.1`). An app with no `start` command (a
static folder) is no longer served at all; give it one, for example:

```json
"start": "python3 -m http.server $PORT --bind 127.0.0.1"
```

Also: if an app's port is already in use by another program (possibly another
user's lodge on the same machine), 0.6 refuses to start the app instead of
serving whatever answers on that port. Pick a free port in its `woltspace.json`.

### 6. Wolt sites

Applies to every wolt with a site. Nothing to move: the site files stay in
`wolt/site/`. Tell the human:

- the site now opens at `http://<wolt>.sites.localhost:<gateway port>/` (the
  gateway port is the lodge port minus 660, e.g. 7117), and old links redirect;
- it's private to them; `woltspace site share <wolt> <email>` lets someone in
  (that only matters remotely, which comes later);
- from outside this machine, sites aren't served yet;
- the built-in About / Memory / Settings pages stay on the lodge, and About
  shows the wolt's `identity.md`.

Inside a site, a page can again fetch its own files and use local storage, but
it can't reach the lodge. Pop-ups and form submits are blocked.

### 7. Container lodges without a named tunnel

Applies if a container lodge relied on the random quick-tunnel address. It is
gone; the lodge is reachable on this machine only. To reach it from elsewhere,
set up a named tunnel with Access (cloudflare skill, `setup.md`), or another way
in the human trusts (a tailnet; add its hostname to `WOLTSPACE_ALLOWED_HOSTS`).

## After

Restart the lodge if `.env` changed (`woltspace stop`, `woltspace start`; tmux
sessions survive). On a native lodge a wolt can run that itself, with the
human's OK. In a container, the wolt can't restart the lodge: the human runs
the restart from the host. Run `woltspace doctor` and read out any warning. Then tell
the human, in one short message, what changed for them: which apps are shared
how, and whether the lodge now checks its own login.

## Rolling back

Installing 0.5.x again restores the old behaviour. Nothing in this migration
deletes data: the `access` block, share lists and the new environment keys are
simply ignored by 0.5. `"public": true` works again on 0.5 if it was left in
the manifest, and 0.5 turns sharing on by default (`WOLTSPACE_SHARING_ENABLED`),
so such an app opens a public quick tunnel the next time it starts. Remove
`"public": true` before rolling back if the app should stay private.
